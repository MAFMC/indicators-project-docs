Discussion 1

* Council members don’t necessarily think in terms of categories of decisions this way.  
* Uncertainty  
* Need to be cautious about more and more info complicating decisions  
* Scientific expert input on how to interpret data and uncertainty and what it means for management  
  * Scientists need to better understand what’s most useful for management  
* Different people prioritize differently   
* Communication to help avoid misinterpretation   
* Indicators can help people get more on the same page  
* Tradeoffs across priorities (e.g., across national standards) \- can disagree on the tradeoffs but not on facts (hopefully)  
* Long vs short term  
* Indicators as supplement  
* Useful when decreasing uncertainty \- not as useful if indicators increase uncertainty  
* Too many indicators \- can cherry pick what suits a desired outcome  
  * Therefore, focus on mgmt decision first, then indicators to go along with it\!  
* Council members have limited time to digest briefing materials and make decisions in the meeting \- can indicators help with that?  
* State agency folks generally think longer term. Fishery stakeholders may be focused more on what the decision means for their bottom line in the immediate future \- can’t think longer term if the short term is bad.  
* Present context with indicators \- what is driving the trend?  
* Sequencing \- get the info out there early enough to influence decisions \- during the meeting is too late \- people have already decided how they will vote  
* Agreed upon indicators as a starting point for discussion  
* Managing bycatch \- could do more  
* Species distribution models to help inform bycatch caps, etc.   
  * Especially with changing distributions  
* Uncertainty with where fish will be in the future \- right now moving to where they are. Similar to aurora example. How much certainty in where the fish will be in the future? Worth investing in XYZ now?  
  * Can CEFI help with this? E.g., cold pool location.  
  * Starting with bottom temperature. Could build indicators from there. Still some reduced work going on.  
  * Seasonal, decadal, regional projections much harder than global.  
* Knock on effects can cause big problems \- hard to predict  
* Retrospective look at emergency regs in the past \- what indicators might have helped?  
* Guidance to the industry even if not regulatory. Heads up on what’s seen in the indicators and on the water feedback on if it seems right. E.g., trends and distribution of bycatch species.

Discussion 2

* All mgmt decisions require more information\!  
* **For catch decisions, Council is constrained by SSC. If there’s a desire to focus on catch decisions, indicators would be at the SSC or assessment level.**   
  * Can’t go higher than SSC’s ABC \- only lower.   
  * What’s useful for the SSC? Or stock assessments.  
  * Indicators as supplemental info to the Council \- help explain or provide context to SSC decisions.  
  * To what extent is SSC already using indicators? Formally or informally.  
  * SSC is largely directly taking what the assessments say.  
  * SSC terms of reference could include consideration of indicators and thresholds.  
* If assessment process or other scientific process warrants re-consideration related to an indicator, that represents a time lag for when it can eventually influence management. E.g., an indicator suggests part of the assessment should be re-evaluated. Need to wait for the next assessment for that to potentially happen.  
* ESPs good place for stock specific indicators.  
* SSC TOR covers sources of uncertainty. But often a laundry list, not prioritized.  
* Example \- recreational effort increasing potentially causing an increase in bycatch. Can help the Council prioritize issues in the implementation plan discussion.   
* SOE indicators are largely not brought forward in later species-specific discussions. Could be helpful to use them more in other parts of the process.   
* Maybe staff should review the SOE indicators before preparing background documents for subsequent meetings \- provide them as context.  
* Changing availability of fish species \- drives what people fish for. Dependence on one or multiple species. Diversity of opportunities in fishing communities.  
* Trends in fishing opportunities could help with some decisions.  
* Tradeoffs of risks vs opportunities.  
  * E.g., scup very high biomass is supporting for-hire in NY. Going from current 300% of target down to 100% of the target would be bad.  
* Overlap (or not) of seasonal openings. Lack of overlap increases pressure on species that are open.  
* Related commercial considerations \- e.g., lower harvest of menhaden for bait puts higher pressure on mackerel.  
* But consideration of opportunities requires consideration of what regs are from all Councils, ASFMC, states, HMS… complicated\!  
* **For measures considerations \- consider opportunities, requires info from other agencies**.  
* Might need to simplify it to something like a lot, some, and few or no opportunities for other species.  
* **Implementation plan \- defining future priorities/needs**  
* Need frequent communication between managers and scientists on what they both need  
  * E.g., research track TORs defined years before the assessment is done  
  * Can also help decide when to stop working on things that won’t be useful\!  
* More opportunities for adjustments  
* **Area based management** \- evaluating effectiveness of current areas.   
* Species and fisheries overlap changing over time \- mismatches can be problematic \- helpful to keep track. Some overlaps are incompatible.  
* User conflict \- e.g., different gear types fishing the same areas. Use conflicts can be driven by changing environment.
